This analysis focuses on the economic feasibility of a zero-waste desalination facility in Cuba, New Mexico.  This consists of government funding, construction costs, and expected revenue help us understand the potential economic benefits of treating brackish groundwater.

## Current Water Conditions in Cuba, NM

As of October 2026, Cuba, New Mexico, was facing a classified D2 drought, with 100% of Sandoval County residents being affected. Residents rely on their local municipal groundwater system for drinking water, with restrictions limiting water usage to essential needs.

## Economic Feasibility Analysis

The Python graph analysis of government funding, estimated construction costs, and expected revenue for the proposed desalination facility, that could be promising to Cuba.

The graph compares three financial categories:

- **Government Funding:** $11.7 million
- **Estimated Construction Cost:** $10 million
- **Expected Revenue:** $13 million

The results show that government funding is greater than the original estimated construction cost by $1.7 million. The facility also has the potential to generate revenue through the sale of high-grade fertilizer and surplus water.

## Projected Water Production

The zero-waste desalination facility is expected to produce 518,000 gallons of potable water per day.

The facility could provide an additional source of clean drinking water while recovering valuable minerals from brackish groundwater.

## Connection to Cuba, New Mexico

Economic feasibility is vital for Cuba, NM as they already have limited resources and cannot take this financial aid for granted. The community also is in a dire need for sustainable and affordable source of clean drinking water.

The proposed desalination facility could address water scarcity while generating local revenue. Minerals extracted from brackish groundwater could be processed into high-grade fertilizer, providing additional economic benefits to the community.

The financial projections demonstrate the economic potential of treating brackish groundwater. However, the estimates come from different years, and operating and maintenance costs would need to be considered to determine long-term profitability.
