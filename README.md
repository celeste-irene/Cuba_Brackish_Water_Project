## Team 6's Project is on Brackish water

Crux: 
Cuba, New Mexico is in a state of emergency due to dwindling water supplies. Fracking and tapping into the village aquifer and the evaluation of the ecological effects of drilling into brackish water is existential as it will decide the water future of the village of Cuba, New Mexico. 

Expected Contributions:
1. Alara Hart: Economic Feasibility and the Current Status of Cuba, New Mexico’s Water Usage
2. Danielle Glikman: Pollution and Depletion of the Water Systems as a Result of Treatment and Fracking 
3. Prime Minister - Ruben Cedric Romero: The Effects of Pumping
4. Celeste Bivens: A General Overview of Cuba, New Mexico’s Aquifer and of Similar Aquifers and Reservoirs Across the State
5. Malaika Williams: Understand the Salinity of Brackish Water

Abstract:
Extreme water conservation is in effect in the village of Cuba, New Mexico. Prolonged drought and dwindling surface water supplies continue to plague the village community as the effects of climate change continue to accelerate. The acceleration of its effect has led the village to make drastic measurements and find new solutions to meet water consumption. Fracking into the aquifer beneath the village and the evaluation of the ecological effects of drilling deep to access brackish water is existential for the village of Cuba. It is expected that the increase of water supply will further increase consumption via induced demand. The research is split into five parts to understand the specifics of feasibility and consequence: one, economic feasibility, as the treatment of water can stimulate growth; two, pollution, if treatment and fracking will further deplete the water systems of the area; three, the effect of pumping itself; four, conduct a comparison and collect general data of similar aquifers and reservoirs across the state; five, to understand the salinity of brackish water. It is hoped that the cross-examination of datasets and analysis can answer if tapping into the village aquifer is truly the answer to water scarcity in Cuba, New Mexico.
